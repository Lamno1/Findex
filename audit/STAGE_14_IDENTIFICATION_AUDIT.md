# Stage 14 — Identification Audit

## 1. Sources of variation, stated precisely

| Layer | Variable | Variation | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual | `digital_payment` (`anydigpayment`) | 0/1 within economy-wave | ~96,711 (2021) / 97,847 (2024) respondents |
| Individual | `formal_borrow` (`fin22a`) | 0/1 within economy-wave | outcome |
| Economy (fixed, time-invariant) | `lowcov_z` | continuous, sign-flipped standardized 2019 coverage | 93 values, identical in both waves |
| Cross-level | `digital_payment × lowcov_z` | product of the above | identified by combining within-economy micro slope with 93 economy-level moderator values |

The interaction coefficient is a single number per wave estimated off exactly 93
economy-level moderator values (fixed effects absorb any economy-level main effect of
coverage itself, which is correct and intentional — the design cannot and does not claim to
identify a main effect of coverage, only how it moderates the payment slope). This is
explicitly acknowledged in the manuscript's Limitations section ("identified by 93
economy-level historical moderator values, not by 194,558 independent observations of
institutional variation") and independently confirmed correct by this review.

## 2. Can composition mechanically generate the pattern?

Tested directly (not merely asserted) in this review:

- **Weighting scheme.** Unweighted LPM reproduces the same sign and similar magnitude
  (`results/stage5_estimation/weight_sensitivity.csv`: -0.0161 in 2021, -0.0143 in 2024,
  both p<0.01) — the interaction is not an artifact of the equal-weighting-by-economy
  estimand choice.
- **Moderator standardization convention.** This review's independent from-scratch
  reconstruction, standardizing coverage at the economy level (93 equally weighted values)
  instead of the pooled-respondent level used throughout the project's own scripts,
  reproduces the same sign and a similar magnitude (-0.0171 vs. -0.0174 in 2021; -0.0157 vs.
  -0.0161 in 2024) — not an artifact of that technical choice either.
- **Single-economy influence.** All 93 (2021) and 93 (2024) leave-one-economy-out
  coefficients are negative (see `STAGE_14_LOO_DIAGNOSTIC.csv`), with a mechanically narrow
  IQR because `w_equal` gives every economy the same aggregate weight (dropping one economy
  removes ≈1/93 of total weight by construction). This rules out single-country influence
  but is not informative about influence by *groups* of economies (region, income tier);
  that check was not performed by the frozen pipeline and was outside the scope of a
  diagnostic-only Stage 14 review (no new primary estimation was run).

**Conclusion: the sign and rough magnitude of the interaction are not mechanically
generated by the specific weighting, standardization, or any single economy.** This is a
genuine internal-validity strength.

## 3. Can economy-level composition explain the pattern via omitted institutional variables?

Not testable with the current design, and this is the identification design's real
limitation (distinct from a computational error): no country-level GDP per capita,
financial-system depth, or digitalization index is interacted with `digital_payment` in the
primary specification, so `lowcov_z` cannot be distinguished, within this design, from any
other economy-level characteristic correlated with 2019 credit-bureau/registry coverage
across the same 93 economies — most obviously general income/financial-development level.
The `SECONDARY_DEPTH` specification (using `depth_credit_info_0_8` instead of coverage)
gives a similarly signed but smaller-magnitude interaction (-0.0111 in 2021, p=0.046;
-0.0107 in 2024, p=0.039) — consistent with either (a) a genuine but depth-independent
coverage channel, or (b) both coverage and depth partly proxying the same underlying
development gradient with different noise levels. The design cannot adjudicate between
these; the manuscript correctly treats depth as "secondary evidence" and does not claim the
distinction is resolved.

## 4. The sample-composition/selection finding (headline identification concern)

Full detail and the coverage-distribution comparison are in
`STAGE_14_INDEPENDENT_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md` §3. Summary for this audit:

- The 2024 baseline "97 economies" is not an accident of which countries Findex visited; it
  is the set where the borrowing/digital-payment **module was administered** (per
  `papers/study_05/PREREGISTRATION.md` §0), a survey-fielding decision plausibly correlated
  with the recipient economy's financial-inclusion/development level.
- 40 economies present in **both** raw 2021 and raw 2024 releases are excluded from the
  93-economy analysis frame, and this excluded set is overwhelmingly high-income (AUS, AUT,
  BEL, CAN, CHE, CHL, CYP, CZE, DEU, DNK, ESP, EST, FIN, FRA, GBR, GRC, HKG, HUN, IRL, ISL,
  ISR, ITA, JPN, KOR, LTU, LVA, MLT, NLD, NOR, NZL, PRT, RUS, SGP, SVK, SVN, SWE, TWN, URY,
  USA), verified in this review to have full (non-missing) 2019 coverage data, mostly at or
  near 100%.
- The 93-economy sample's institutional-coverage IQR (11.7–60.7) is compressed relative to
  the pool of economies with valid 2019 coverage data that are excluded from the frame
  (1.2–92.6); 24 of the 34 economies worldwide with coverage ≥95% are excluded.

**Identification consequence:** the interaction is estimated on a population whose range of
institutional environments has already been truncated by a mechanism (module
administration) plausibly related to the very construct being tested. This does not
invalidate the sign or the internal computation, but it means "boundary condition,"
"transportability," and "generalises" language in the manuscript describes a narrower
population than a reader would infer from "93 economies common to the 2021 and 2024 Global
Findex waves" alone. This is this review's single strongest, non-duplicative addition to
the existing Stage 9/12/13 record (see `STAGE_14_REVIEWER_INDEPENDENCE_NOTE.md`).

## 5. Formal distinction (Section 22 of the brief)

- **Q1 (are the reported numbers correct)?** YES — independently reconstructed from raw
  files with a different (economy-level) standardization convention and reproduced the same
  sign, similar magnitude, and matching cluster p-values.
- **Q2 (is the statistical design implemented correctly)?** YES, with two precision caveats:
  (a) the moderator standardization is computed at the pooled-respondent level, not the
  economy level, which is a defensible but non-obvious choice that should be disclosed; (b)
  the "wild-cluster bootstrap" is a null-imposed cluster-score Rademacher variant with a
  fixed (non-re-estimated) standard error per replicate, not the classical
  Cameron-Gelbach-Miller refit-based wild-cluster-restricted bootstrap; both are legitimate
  but under-specified in the manuscript's plain-language description.
- **Q3 (does the interpretation exceed what the design can establish)?** Mostly NO for the
  causal/mechanism language, which is already tightly hedged (see
  `STAGE_14_CLAIM_LANGUAGE_REVIEW.md`). Partially YES for the population scope implied by
  "93 economies common to the 2021 and 2024 Global Findex waves": the design can establish
  the interaction for a population that has already been filtered on module-administration
  grounds correlated with financial development, and the manuscript does not disclose that
  filter's substantive content, which risks a reader over-extending "transportability" to
  the full global range of credit-information environments.
