# Stage 14 — Alternative Explanations Matrix

Compatibility is judged against the observed pattern: positive `digital_payment` main
effect and negative `digital_payment × lowcov_z` interaction, present in both 2021 and 2024,
sign-robust to leave-one-economy-out and to weighting choice.

| # | Alternative explanation | Compatible with current data? | Evidence against | Evidence supporting | Can current design distinguish it from the stated interpretation? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Account-access selection (who even has a bank account to borrow through) | Yes, fully compatible | None found; account-conditioning (SPECB) changes the coefficient in opposite directions across waves (attenuates 2021, strengthens 2024), which is itself unexplained | ~3% of `account_fin`=1 in 2021 is payment/card-derived, creating direct construction overlap with `digital_payment`; account main effect is large and significant in both waves | No. The design has no application/approval data and cannot separate access-selection from an information channel. |
| 2 | General financial-system development / income level (not credit-information specifically) | Yes, fully compatible | Depth-based secondary specification gives a similarly signed, smaller-magnitude interaction, weakly consistent with a shared underlying gradient rather than two independent institutional signals | Coverage and depth are correlated by construction (both World Bank "Getting Credit" sub-indices); no country-level GDP/financial-depth interaction is included to net this out | No. No income/financial-depth interaction term exists in the primary or secondary specifications. |
| 3 | Country-level omitted institutional characteristics (rule of law, regulation, competition in lending) | Yes, fully compatible | None found | Economy fixed effects absorb *level* differences but cannot absorb differences in *how* an omitted institutional variable moderates the payment slope | No. Only 93 cross-sectional moderator values are available; omitted moderators cannot be ruled out with one candidate moderator. |
| 4 | Measurement error in the 2019 coverage moderator (incl. legacy Doing Business data-integrity questions) | Compatible in principle | Coverage and depth indices behave consistently with each other, suggesting the underlying data are not grossly corrupted for this project's sample as a whole | The moderator's source series (World Bank "Getting Credit" / Doing Business) was discontinued in 2021 following a data-integrity review of a subset of country scores; whether any of the 93 sample economies were among the affected scores was not independently verified in this review | Partially. A country-by-country check against WB corrigenda was outside this review's scope; it should be a specific pre-Stage-15 action. |
| 5 | Digitalization/product-mix differences across economies (e.g., mobile money dominant vs. card dominant) | Yes, fully compatible | None found | `anydigpayment` is an aggregate flag spanning multiple product types (`STAGE_3A2` construct audit); economies differ substantially in which products dominate | No. The construct is a single binary flag; product-type heterogeneity cannot be separated within it. |
| 6 | Persistent survey-sample composition (module-administration selection correlated with the moderator) | Yes, fully compatible, and independently confirmed in this review | None found; this review directly verified the excluded economies have full, mostly near-100%, coverage data, ruling out "missing data" as the exclusion reason | 40 economies present in both raw waves are excluded from the 93-economy frame and are overwhelmingly high-income/high-coverage; the 93-sample's coverage IQR is compressed relative to the excluded pool | No. The estimation sample is fixed by the frozen 93-economy manifest; the design cannot test sensitivity to including the excluded high-income economies without violating the freeze. |
| 7 | Lender-side information usability (whether lenders can technically ingest payment records even where they exist) | Compatible, and the manuscript treats this as an open, unresolved explanation | None found; no lender-side data exist in Findex | Plausible institutionally (retail credit-scoring infrastructure varies widely) but entirely speculative absent lender data | No. Findex is a household survey; no lender/application/underwriting data are observed. |
| 8 | Supply-side credit rationing (lenders restrict formal credit in thin-information economies regardless of applicant payment records) | Compatible | None found | Consistent with standard credit-rationing theory in thin-information environments | No. Realised borrowing conflates demand, selection, and supply; the survey cannot separate them, a limitation the manuscript states explicitly. |
| 9 | Firm-vs-retail lending technology differences (the paper's own stated boundary-condition interpretation) | Compatible, and this is the manuscript's own preferred framing | None found | Directly consistent with the firm-level benchmark using a different unit, outcome, and moderator | No more than any other item on this list — the manuscript correctly does not claim this explanation is established over the others; it is offered as the least mechanism-committal reading. |
| 10 | Realised-borrowing outcome mixing heterogeneous credit products with different information requirements | Compatible; not resolved by files available to this review | Project's own construct audits describe `fin22a` only as "formal borrowing from a bank or other formal financial institution," without confirming or denying multi-product aggregation | Global Findex's borrowing indicators are commonly constructed as aggregates across products in other Findex applications, but this was not independently verified against the codebook here (no internet access used in this review) | Unresolved — flagged as an open verification item for Stage 15, not asserted as a confirmed weakness. |

## Reading the matrix

Items 1, 2, 5, 6, and 10 are, in this reviewer's judgment, the alternatives that remain
**most** observationally compatible with the data as currently structured, because the
design has no lever (control, interaction, or subsample) capable of ruling any of them out.
Item 6 (persistent, moderator-correlated sample selection) is the one this review adds most
substantively to the existing record; items 7-9 are the mechanism-level explanations the
manuscript itself already declines to adjudicate among, correctly. No alternative in this
matrix is assigned a higher or lower prior than the others beyond what the evidence
columns support; none is elevated to a finding.
